# BUSINESS PLAN & MARKET OPPORTUNITY
## AI-Powered Transportation Management, Predictive Fleet Intelligence & Digital Freight Marketplace

**Business Focus:** Transportation Technology, Fleet Management, Artificial Intelligence and Freight Marketplace  
**Target Market:** Saudi Arabia, with potential expansion into other GCC markets  
**Business Model:** Free Basic TMS + Paid Enterprise Software + Transaction-Based Freight Marketplace

---

# 1. Executive Summary

The transportation and logistics industry depends on efficient vehicle utilization, reliable trip coordination, competitive freight pricing, and timely access to transportation capacity. However, transporters and freight customers often operate through disconnected systems, making it difficult to identify available vehicles, coordinate shipments, and maximize the value of existing fleet capacity.

One significant challenge is the empty return trip. After delivering goods to a destination, a truck may return without cargo because the transporter cannot identify a suitable shipment for the return journey. The transporter still incurs operating costs, while a customer travelling along a compatible route may be paying for a separate dedicated truck.

Our business aims to address this inefficiency by building an integrated, AI-powered transportation ecosystem that connects transporters, fleet operators, and customers requiring freight transportation.

The platform combines three core capabilities:

1. **Transportation Management System (TMS):** A free basic waybill-generation solution for transporter onboarding, supported by paid advanced modules for businesses requiring broader operational management.
2. **GPS Integration and AI-Powered Transportation Intelligence:** Vehicle tracking, trip visibility, estimated arrival times, predictive availability, and intelligent route analysis.
3. **Digital Freight Marketplace:** A platform that connects suitable transportation capacity with customer freight requirements, including potential return-trip opportunities.

Our strategy is to make the basic transportation solution accessible to transporters at no cost, encouraging platform adoption and authorized GPS integration. As participating transporters generate waybills and record trips, the platform can build a more comprehensive view of vehicle movements and transportation patterns.

Using this information, the system can estimate when and where vehicles may become available and identify potential matches with customer demand. Transporters can then review suitable freight opportunities and accept shipments according to their availability, vehicle suitability, pricing, and operating requirements.

The business will generate revenue through paid TMS implementation, recurring software subscriptions, and commissions on eligible completed marketplace transactions.

The long-term vision is to transform transportation data into actionable freight opportunities, helping transporters improve fleet utilization while providing customers with access to more competitive transportation options.

# 3. Market Problem and Business Opportunity

## 3.1 Empty Return Trips

Transporters frequently complete deliveries without having a suitable return shipment arranged. This can result in vehicles travelling empty, reducing their productive utilization and limiting the revenue generated from their operating capacity.

The transporter may have an available vehicle, a known route, and an estimated return time, but lack visibility into customer freight requirements that could fit the journey.

### Our opportunity

Use TMS records, GPS data, and AI-assisted availability prediction to identify potential return-trip opportunities and connect them with customers requiring compatible transportation.

The objective is to improve the likelihood of finding suitable loads for return journeys and generate additional revenue where commercially and operationally feasible.

## 3.2 High Cost of Dedicated Transportation

Customers often need to arrange dedicated trucks for individual shipments. Depending on route, timing, cargo, and availability, dedicated transportation can result in higher costs.

Customers may also need to contact multiple transporters to obtain quotations and confirm availability.

### Our opportunity

Provide customers with access to a broader network of transporters and potential vehicle availability. Where an existing vehicle movement can accommodate a compatible shipment, the customer may benefit from a more competitive rate.

Any savings will depend on the actual route, vehicle availability, cargo requirements, pricing, and successful booking.

## 3.3 Fragmented Transportation Operations

Transportation information may be distributed across spreadsheets, phone calls, messaging applications, GPS platforms, waybills, and separate business systems.

This fragmentation makes it difficult to maintain a unified view of:

- Vehicle locations and movements.
- Current and upcoming trips.
- Estimated vehicle availability.
- Customer freight requirements.
- Potential return loads.
- Vehicle suitability and capacity.
- Shipment progress and operational performance.

### Our opportunity

Create a connected transportation ecosystem that brings together essential trip records, authorized GPS information, customer demand, and intelligent freight matching.

## 3.4 Limited Visibility into Future Vehicle Availability

Traditional tracking primarily explains where a vehicle is currently located. However, knowing where a truck is does not automatically reveal when it will become available for another shipment.

### Our opportunity

Use historical trip information, current GPS movement, delivery schedules, and operational data to estimate when and where a vehicle may become available.

This shifts the platform from simple vehicle tracking toward predictive transportation planning.

# 4. Our Proposed Solution

The platform will combine software, vehicle data, predictive intelligence, and marketplace connectivity into one integrated transportation solution.

## 4.1 Free Basic Transportation Management System

We will provide transporters with a free basic module for generating and managing waybills.

The basic module will serve as the primary entry point into the platform, reducing the initial cost barrier and encouraging transporters to digitize essential transportation activities.

### Core capabilities

- Basic waybill generation.
- Essential trip and transportation records.
- Vehicle and transporter information.
- Supported GPS integration.
- Access to the freight marketplace.
- Basic trip visibility and operational information.

The exact scope of the free module will be defined according to product capabilities and operating costs.

### Strategic value

The free module is intended to accelerate adoption and build a network of participating transporters. As the network grows, the platform may gain access to more authorized route and trip information, creating better opportunities to connect transportation capacity with customer demand.

The basic solution is therefore an adoption strategy rather than a standalone revenue product.

## 4.2 Paid Advanced TMS Solutions

Transporters requiring more comprehensive operational management can upgrade to paid versions of the TMS.

Potential modules include:

- Advanced transportation operations.
- Fleet and vehicle management.
- Driver management and mobile applications.
- Preventive and corrective maintenance.
- Workshop management.
- Inventory and spare-parts management.
- Procurement.
- Fuel and tyre management.
- Finance and cost management.
- Operational reporting and performance dashboards.
- Advanced integrations and customized workflows.

The paid offering can be configured according to fleet size, business complexity, number of users, modules required, and implementation scope.

This allows the business to serve both smaller transporters needing basic tools and larger fleet operators requiring more advanced systems.

## 4.3 GPS Integration

GPS integration will provide the data foundation for fleet visibility and predictive transportation intelligence.

Subject to provider capabilities, connectivity, and appropriate authorization, the platform can receive information about vehicle location, movement, and trip progress.

Combined with waybill and operational data, this information can support:

- Live or periodically updated vehicle location.
- Trip progress monitoring.
- Estimated arrival times.
- Route deviation alerts.
- Vehicle movement history.
- Potential availability windows.
- Route and fleet utilization analysis.

GPS data quality and update frequency will determine the reliability of these features.

## 4.4 AI-Powered Transportation Intelligence

Artificial intelligence will be used to analyze transportation data and generate useful predictions and recommendations.

The platform will progressively develop capabilities to estimate vehicle availability, identify compatible return trips, recommend transporters, and improve matching decisions.

The AI capability will develop as sufficient reliable historical data becomes available. Initial functionality may use rules, route logic, and conventional forecasting before progressing to more advanced machine-learning models.

## 4.5 Digital Freight Marketplace

The marketplace will connect customers who require freight transportation with participating transporters who have suitable capacity.

Customers can submit requirements such as:

- Pickup location.
- Delivery destination.
- Cargo type and dimensions, where relevant.
- Required vehicle type and capacity.
- Pickup and delivery schedule.
- Preferred price or quotation requirements.

The platform will identify potential matches based on route, availability, vehicle suitability, timing, and commercial terms.

Transporters will be able to review opportunities and confirm whether they can accept the shipment.

The marketplace will earn commission from eligible completed transactions under the agreed commercial model.

# 5. AI and Technology Strategy

## 5.1 AI-Powered Predictive Vehicle Availability

The platform aims to predict not only where a truck is, but also when and where it may become available for its next trip.

The prediction engine can consider:

- Current GPS location and direction.
- Active waybill and trip details.
- Planned origin and destination.
- Historical route travel times.
- Estimated arrival time.
- Expected unloading duration, where known.
- Vehicle type and capacity.
- Historical trip patterns.
- Known delays and traffic information, where available.
- Existing bookings and schedule conflicts.

For example, if a truck is travelling from Dammam to Riyadh, the system may estimate when it will arrive in Riyadh and when it could potentially accept another shipment.

The system can then search for compatible customer requests along the return route.

Predicted availability is an estimate, not a confirmed booking. Transporter confirmation will be required before the vehicle is treated as available for a new shipment.

## 5.2 AI-Powered Return-Trip Matching

The matching engine will identify freight requirements that may fit an upcoming vehicle movement.

Matching criteria may include:

- Origin and destination compatibility.
- Pickup and delivery windows.
- Vehicle type and capacity.
- Cargo restrictions and handling requirements.
- Estimated availability.
- Additional route distance.
- Expected travel time.
- Price and commercial terms.
- Transporter preferences.

The system can prioritize matches that are operationally suitable and potentially attractive to both parties.

Over time, completed transaction data can help improve recommendations by identifying which types of matches are most likely to be accepted and completed successfully.

## 5.3 Intelligent Route and Trip Optimization

The platform can analyze route compatibility and identify opportunities that fit within existing transportation schedules.

Potential features include:

- Alternative route recommendations.
- Additional-distance estimation.
- Pickup and delivery window compatibility.
- Identification of potential route conflicts.
- Route-based demand analysis.
- Recommendations for suitable return loads.

Where reliable mapping and traffic data are available, these can be incorporated into estimated travel times and route recommendations.

## 5.4 AI-Powered Freight Recommendations

Customers can receive recommendations for transporters and vehicles that appear suitable for their shipment requirements.

Recommendations can be ranked according to:

- Route compatibility.
- Vehicle suitability.
- Estimated availability.
- Quoted rate.
- Expected delivery time.
- Historical service performance, where sufficient data exists.
- Customer and transporter preferences.

This can reduce the time required to find suitable transport options and simplify the booking process.

## 5.5 Predictive Demand Analytics

As the platform accumulates sufficient reliable data, analytics models can identify recurring transportation patterns.

Potential capabilities include:

- Forecasting demand for particular routes.
- Identifying routes with frequent empty return journeys.
- Identifying vehicle categories with high demand.
- Predicting periods of potential vehicle availability.
- Supporting fleet planning.
- Highlighting routes with strong marketplace potential.

These insights can help the business prioritize expansion and focus resources on routes where the marketplace can create measurable value.

## 5.6 AI-Assisted Pricing Recommendations

The platform may introduce a pricing recommendation engine using historical completed transactions and relevant operating factors.

Potential inputs include route, distance, vehicle type, cargo, timing, historical rates, and additional distance required.

The system can recommend a price range for consideration by the transporter and customer. The final commercial price remains subject to the parties' agreement.

## 5.7 Automated Notifications

The platform can generate alerts when:

- A truck is predicted to become available.
- A compatible freight request is identified.
- A vehicle approaches its destination.
- A shipment is delayed.
- A transporter receives a new opportunity.
- A trip is completed and requires confirmation.

Notifications can be delivered through the platform and supported mobile or messaging integrations.

## 5.8 Technology Architecture

The intended technology architecture includes:

- TMS application and operational database.
- GPS provider integrations and data ingestion.
- Route and trip processing services.
- Freight marketplace and booking workflows.
- AI and predictive analytics services.
- Notification and alert services.
- Customer and transporter interfaces.
- Reporting and analytics dashboards.
- Access controls, audit logs, and data security mechanisms.

The implementation will be phased. Predictive and AI capabilities will depend on data availability, model performance, integration reliability, and operational validation.

# 6. How the Platform Works

The end-to-end operating model consists of the following stages.

### Stage 1 — Transporter Onboarding

Transporters register and begin using the free basic waybill solution.

### Stage 2 — GPS Integration

Supported GPS systems are connected with appropriate authorization, allowing the platform to receive relevant vehicle movement information.

### Stage 3 — Trip Data Collection

The platform combines available waybill, vehicle, route, and trip information to build visibility into transportation activity.

### Stage 4 — Predictive Availability

AI-assisted models estimate when and where vehicles may become available based on current and historical information.

### Stage 5 — Customer Onboarding

Customers submit freight requirements, including origin, destination, cargo, vehicle type, and timing.

### Stage 6 — Intelligent Matching

The platform identifies potentially suitable transportation options and recommends compatible matches.

### Stage 7 — Transporter Confirmation

The transporter reviews the shipment details, confirms availability, and agrees to the commercial terms.

### Stage 8 — Shipment Execution

The transporter completes the shipment, with operational status updates recorded through supported workflows.

### Stage 9 — Transaction Completion

The platform records the completed transaction and applies the agreed commission to eligible bookings.

### Stage 10 — Continuous Improvement

Completed trips, acceptance rates, delays, and transaction outcomes provide feedback to improve future matching and predictive recommendations.

# 7. Business Model and Revenue Streams

The business combines software adoption, recurring subscription revenue, and transaction-based marketplace revenue.

## 7.1 Revenue Stream 1 — TMS Implementation

We provide TMS implementation services for customers requiring configured operational software.

The current financial planning model assumes an implementation fee of SAR 40,000 per new customer.

Implementation services may include configuration, setup, data preparation, training, and deployment according to the agreed scope.

## 7.2 Revenue Stream 2 — Paid TMS Subscription

Transporters requiring advanced capabilities can subscribe to paid TMS modules.

The current financial planning model assumes a subscription fee of SAR 1,000 per customer per month.

Actual commercial packages may vary based on modules, users, fleet size, integrations, customization, and support requirements.

## 7.3 Revenue Stream 3 — Marketplace Commission

The marketplace earns commission on eligible completed freight transactions.

The current financial model assumes:

- Commission rate: 3% of the eligible freight transaction value.
- Average freight transaction value: SAR 1,500.
- Commission per eligible transaction: SAR 45.
- Assumed activity: 10 completed trips per customer per month.
- Modeled commission revenue: SAR 450 per customer per month after the applicable one-time free-trip allowance has been used.

The first 10 completed trips are treated as a one-time free allowance under the current planning assumption. The final commercial terms must specify how the allowance applies and when commissions become payable.

## 7.4 Strategic Role of Each Revenue Stream

| Revenue Stream | Strategic Purpose |
|---|---|
| Free basic TMS | Build transporter adoption and network participation |
| TMS implementation | Generate project-based revenue |
| Paid TMS subscription | Generate recurring software revenue |
| Marketplace commission | Monetize completed freight transactions |
| Potential advanced analytics services | Create future monetization opportunities as the platform matures |

The business will not depend entirely on selling software licenses or solely on marketplace transactions. The combined model creates multiple ways to monetize the transportation ecosystem.

# 14. Key Risks and Mitigation

## 14.1 Insufficient Transporter Supply

A marketplace needs enough suitable vehicles on the relevant routes.

**Mitigation:** Start with focused routes and onboard transporters whose vehicle movements align with known customer requirements.

## 14.2 Insufficient Customer Demand

Transporter adoption alone does not guarantee transactions.

**Mitigation:** Prioritize customers with recurring freight demand and validate route-level demand before expanding the network.

## 14.3 GPS Data Quality

GPS updates may be delayed, incomplete, or unavailable.

**Mitigation:** Monitor data reliability, use supported integrations, combine GPS with TMS records, and require transporter confirmation.

## 14.4 Prediction Accuracy

Estimated arrival times and future availability may differ from actual conditions.

**Mitigation:** Treat AI output as a recommendation, measure prediction accuracy, account for uncertainty, and avoid confirming bookings without operational approval.

## 14.5 Commercial Compatibility

A potential match may be unsuitable because of vehicle type, cargo restrictions, delivery windows, additional distance, or price.

**Mitigation:** Apply compatibility rules and confirm all material commercial and operational requirements before booking.

## 14.6 Customer Retention

Customers may stop using the marketplace if it does not provide reliable availability or competitive pricing.

**Mitigation:** Track repeat bookings, completed trips, service quality, and measurable customer value.

## 14.7 Data Protection and Security

GPS, route, vehicle, and customer information must be handled responsibly.

**Mitigation:** Obtain necessary authorization, define permitted data use, implement access controls, and maintain appropriate security and audit processes.
