# FIVE-YEAR FINANCIAL PLAN & REVENUE PROJECTIONS
## TMS Implementation, Subscription Services & Freight Marketplace

**Projection Period:** Year 1 to Year 5
**Currency:** Saudi Riyal (SAR)
**Document Purpose:** Financial Planning, Investment Evaluation and Business Decision-Making

---

# 1. Introduction

This document presents the five-year financial plan and revenue projections for a business operating across three complementary revenue streams: Transportation Management System (TMS) implementation, recurring TMS subscription services, and a digital freight marketplace.

The financial plan outlines the expected costs, revenue generation, customer acquisition targets, and operating profitability over five years. It is designed to provide a clear understanding of the funding required to launch the business, the resources needed to support expansion, and the anticipated transition from initial operating losses to positive operating profitability.

The business will initially focus on establishing the technology platform, building the operational and sales teams, acquiring customers, and developing market presence. In subsequent years, the strategy will emphasize customer acquisition, increased platform adoption, recurring subscription income, marketplace transaction growth, and improved operational efficiency.

The projections are based on defined pricing assumptions, planned customer acquisition, estimated employee-related expenses, marketing budgets, technology infrastructure, and customer support requirements. The financial model assumes that implementation revenue will support early-stage operations while subscription and marketplace commission revenue progressively become the primary sources of recurring income.

This document is organized into five annual financial plans, followed by the initial investment requirement, financial assumptions, and a consolidated five-year financial summary.

All amounts are preliminary estimates and should be validated against the final staffing plan, commercial agreements, operating requirements, and actual customer acquisition performance.

# 8. Revenue Model & Financial Assumptions

The five-year financial forecast uses the following commercial and operating assumptions.

## 8.1 TMS Implementation Revenue

- Implementation fee: SAR 40,000 per new customer.
- Year 1: 10 new customers.
- Year 2: 20 new customers.
- Year 3: 30 new customers.
- Year 4: 40 new customers.
- Year 5: No new implementations budgeted.

The cumulative implementation customer base is projected to reach 100 customers by the end of Year 4.

## 8.2 TMS Subscription Revenue

- Monthly subscription fee: SAR 1,000 per customer.
- New customers contribute an assumed six months of subscription revenue during their acquisition year.
- Existing customers contribute 12 months of subscription revenue where applicable.
- Year 5 assumes 100 active customers paying for the full year.

The forecast assumes customers remain subscribed according to the revenue model. Actual revenue will depend on implementation timing, customer activation, retention, and subscription renewals.

## 8.3 Marketplace Commission Revenue

- Commission rate: 3% of eligible completed freight transactions.
- Assumed average freight transaction value: SAR 1,500.
- Commission per eligible transaction: SAR 45.
- Assumed monthly activity: 10 completed trips per customer.
- Assumed commission revenue at the modeled monthly activity level: SAR 450 per customer per month after the one-time free-trip allowance has been used.
- New marketplace customers contribute an assumed average of 5.5 commission-earning months during their acquisition year.
- Existing marketplace customers contribute 12 months of commission revenue.

The first 10 completed trips are treated as a one-time free allowance per customer. Subsequent eligible completed transactions are assumed to generate commission.

The marketplace revenue forecast is particularly sensitive to actual completed trip volumes, customer activity, acquisition timing, and retention.

## 8.4 Customer Acquisition Targets

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Year 1 | 10 | 10 | 100 | 100 |
| Year 2 | 20 | 30 | 200 | 300 |
| Year 3 | 30 | 60 | 300 | 600 |
| Year 4 | 40 | 100 | 400 | 1,000 |
| Year 5 | 0 | 100 | 0 | 1,000 |

These are planning targets rather than confirmed customer commitments.

## 8.5 Expense Assumptions

The expense projections include employee-related costs, sales commissions where budgeted, office rent and facilities, digital marketing, branding, travel and field sales, technology infrastructure, general administration, and customer retention and support.

The projections assume that operating costs can be managed within the stated annual budgets while the customer base expands.

Digital marketing and branding are treated as separate expense categories. Digital marketing includes SEO, social media, and Google Ads. Branding includes offline promotional and brand-building activities.

# 9. Financial Interpretation & Business Outlook

## 9.1 Initial Investment and Early-Stage Operations

Years 1 and 2 are projected to incur operating losses as the business invests in staffing, technology infrastructure, market entry, and customer acquisition. The initial funding requirement is intended to support the first two years of operations and the additional establishment and acquisition costs specified in the investment budget.

## 9.2 Transition to Profitability

Year 3 is projected to achieve positive annual operating profit as implementation revenue and recurring revenue grow. Years 4 and 5 are projected to benefit from the larger customer base and continued marketplace commission revenue.

## 9.3 Recurring Revenue Potential

TMS subscriptions and marketplace commissions provide recurring revenue opportunities. Their contribution to the overall financial performance will depend on customer retention, subscription renewals, and completed freight transactions.

## 9.4 Operational Efficiency

The Year 5 model assumes lower customer acquisition expenses and continued servicing of the existing customer base. This approach may improve operating margins if the business maintains its customers and revenue levels without requiring significant additional staffing or infrastructure expenditure.

## 9.5 Key Financial Risks

The projections may be affected by:

- Customer acquisition being slower than planned.
- Customer churn or subscription cancellations.
- Lower-than-expected freight transaction volumes.
- Delays in customer onboarding or platform adoption.
- Higher employee, technology, marketing, or support costs.
- The need to continue sales and marketing expenditure in Year 5 to maintain the customer base.

These factors should be reviewed periodically against actual financial performance.
